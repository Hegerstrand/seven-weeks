# Team Learning Model

Status: **v0.2 - full coverage pass.** This explains why spending a person-day on Coordinate,
Align, Learn, Reflect, or Demo (instead of Work) actually makes the team better - not just
flavour text on the Team State tracks in [../rules/rulebook.md](../rules/rulebook.md) §4/§7.

Bourdieu lives in [habitus-model.md](./habitus-model.md). This file covers **Vygotsky** and
**Wenger**, and audits whether the basics are actually all in the game.

## The core claim
Work makes the project move forward. Everything else makes the *team* better at doing that
work - and each activity does it through a specific, named mechanism, not generic "team
building."

| Activity | Wenger / Vygotsky mechanism | What it actually does |
|---|---|---|
| **Coordinate** | Wenger's **mutual engagement** - the ongoing relationship that turns a group of people into a real community of practice | Builds **Trust**, this game's stand-in for psychological safety (see below). Mutual engagement is what lets people disagree, admit mistakes, or ask for help. |
| **Align** | Wenger's **joint enterprise** - a goal the group has actually agreed to, not one just handed down | Builds shared understanding of *why*, which is what keeps **Adaptability** working when the *what* changes (§4). |
| **Learn** | Vygotsky's **zone of proximal development & scaffolding** - a more knowledgeable other helps a learner do something they couldn't do alone, then steps back as it's learned | Transfers Knowledge and grows **Capability** (§8) - this is scaffolding, played out mechanically. |
| **Reflect** | Wenger's **reification** (turning experience into something shareable) plus Vygotsky's **internalization** (turning outside experience into inside skill) | Grows **Shared Practice** and **Adaptability** - an After Action Review is the step that turns one person's experience into the team's shared memory. |
| **Demo** | Wenger's **boundary encounter** - showing the work to people outside the practice, which tests whether the team's actual skill matches what it thought it had | Produces honest feedback - sometimes the news that the team is behind where it thought it was. That's exactly the information the Zone of Proximal Development needs. |
| **Workshop** | Wenger's **joint enterprise** carried out directly - this is the "useful coordination" half of the old Working/Record Meeting split; the empty-ritual half is gone (§9, v0.28) | Grows **Adaptability**, unconditionally - no no-op version to fall back on any more. |
| **Celebrate** | Wenger's **reification**, pointed at the team rather than the work - marking an achievement turns "stuff we did" into a shared story the team can hold about itself | The only thing that pushes back on the weekly grind. **Deliberately not "rest":** nobody rests *at* work. Teams sustain themselves across a hard project by shipping something and saying so - which is why it pays **+3** in a week a feature was banked, and only +2 otherwise. |

## Vygotsky - the basics, and where each one lives

**1. The zone of proximal development.** The gap between what a learner can do alone
and what they can do with help. → **Learn**, plus the rule that **a Knowledge received this week
cannot be taught this week** (§8). Turn the card sideways when it arrives. Before that rule a
Knowledge could cross the whole team in one week - A teaches B on Monday, B teaches C on
Tuesday - which asserts that *being told* a thing and *being able to teach it* are the same
state. The gap between them is the zone.

**2. The more knowledgeable other (MKO).** Someone who already has the skill scaffolds the
learner, then withdraws support. → **Learn takes one of the week's five days** (§8), and the
teacher takes **+1 Capital** for it (§5.1). Scaffolding is priced as opportunity: the day spent
teaching is a day the tower does not rise.

> Two harder versions were modelled and dropped. Charging two of the five days outright
> **breaks the budget**, dropping a well-played invested game to ~16 Work against the 18 the
> project needs. Barring the teacher from Work for the rest of the week landed at ~20, but it
> charged for teaching twice - once in the day, again in the restriction - which made the best
> teacher the worst person to teach.

**3. Mediation - thinking is done *through* cultural tools.** Vygotsky's most under-used idea
here: people don't act on the world directly, they act through instruments (physical tools and
sign systems), and the tool reshapes the thinking, not just the speed. → **The Item** - the
prop the character physically holds. It is a mediating artifact, and it's the reason Item is a
separate part of the figure from Torso and Head. → Also **Shared Practice**: a team's shared
language and routines are Vygotsky's *sign* system, which is why the track rewards doing the
same thing twice rather than making any single act stronger.

**4. The general genetic law: every function appears twice** - first *between* people
(interpsychological), then *inside* the person (intrapsychological). → This is exactly
**Knowledge → Shared Practice**: an individual's Knowledge is taught, taught again, and at
three holders it stops being anybody's private property and becomes the team's (§7 Learn).
The game's knowledge rule is a direct implementation of this law, and it's worth knowing that
the "3 holders" threshold is where "between people" turns into "in the practice."

**5. Internalization.** Outside experience becomes inside capability. → **Reflect**, and its
condition that you can only Reflect on a week in which something actually happened.

## Wenger - the basics, and where each one lives

**1. The three dimensions of a community of practice:**

| Dimension | In the game |
|---|---|
| **Mutual engagement** | Coordinate → Trust |
| **Joint enterprise** | Align → Adaptability |
| **Shared repertoire** | The Shared Practice track. At 4-5, the second and every later **Work** card in a week produces +1 extra - the team has a routine for the thing it does most. At 0-1 each of those repeats produces 1 less: nothing is routine, so every one is reinvented. Its Habit is literally named *Shared Repertoire*. |

**2. The participation ↔ reification duality.** Wenger's central claim: practice is made by
two things in tension - *participating* (doing the work together, tacit, in the moment) and
*reifying* (freezing experience into an artifact: a document, a routine, a name for a thing).
Neither alone is enough. → **Participation** is Work, Coordinate, Learn. **Reification** is
Reflect and Demo, and the physical **Feature Tower** - the tower is the team's experience
turned into an object you can point at.

**3. Legitimate peripheral participation** (Lave & Wenger). Newcomers don't sit and watch;
they do real, low-stakes work at the edge of the practice from day one, and move inward. →
**Onboarding (§12)**: a new character works *immediately*, at −1 output, and earns no Capital
until onboarding is paid off. That's LPP precisely - legitimate (real work, from the start),
peripheral (reduced effect, no standing yet). It's also why "rushed onboarding" is a real
option rather than a punishment: peripheral participation is how it's supposed to start.

**4. Boundary encounters and brokering.** Practice meets other practice at its edges;
brokers carry it across. → **Demo** is the boundary encounter with the customer. **NEW TEAM
MEMBER** is a broker arriving with outside Knowledge.

**5. Identity - learning as becoming.** For late Wenger, learning isn't acquiring
information, it's becoming a certain kind of person within a community. → **Capital** (§5.1)
and the habitus figure together: who you are becoming in this team's eyes. This is where
Wenger and Bourdieu meet, and the game runs them on the same track.

## The Ledger, row by row - what each one is actually citing
The Ledger has two columns and they do different jobs. The **left** is what the team *does*,
in plain language. The **right** is what it has *become*, named after the construct it
implements, with the source printed on the board.

| Track | Habit at the ring | Source | The claim being modelled |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trust** | Psychological Safety | Edmondson 1999, *ASQ* 44(2) 350-383 | A shared belief that interpersonal risk is safe. It is the most-studied enabling condition in team-learning research - hence `Trust ≤ 1 → Learn does nothing`. |
| **Capability** | **Who Knows What** | Wegner 1986; Lewis 2003 - *transactive memory* | A group memory made of who holds which expertise, plus the metamemory to find it. Exactly what a Knowledge reaching **3 holders** produces. |
| **Shared Practice** | Shared Repertoire | Lave & Wenger 1991; Wenger 1998 | The communal resources - routines, tools, language - a practice builds. Hence repeated Work pays at `≥4` and costs at `≤1`: a repertoire is exactly what makes the second one cheaper than the first. |
| **Adaptability** | **Team Reflexivity** | West 1996 | Teams that overtly reflect on objectives and processes, *and change them*, outperform those that don't. Reflect grows the track; the track is the capacity to change the plan. |
| **Standing** | **Authorized Voice** | Bourdieu 1991, *Language and Symbolic Power* | An utterance carries force from the speaker's position, not its content. Hence the Demo gate is **relative to the team average**, not absolute. |

Two of these were renamed after an audit found them ungrounded. *Full Capability* and
*Battle-Tested* were consultancy vocabulary and folklore respectively - they described real
mechanics with invented words, which is exactly the failure mode this document exists to catch.

### The Ledger's structure is itself a claim
The most defensible theoretical move in the Ledger isn't any single row - it's **the flip**. A
track walks to a threshold, turns over, and becomes permanent and free. That is
**institutionalisation**: repeated action hardening into a durable disposition that no longer
costs effort. In this game's own vocabulary it is the team's **collective habitus**, which is
why the Habit cancel route (§6.1) costs nothing while Adapt costs the same every single time.
Adapt is coping. A Habit is having changed.

And this rule is exact Bourdieu, arrived at physically before it was named:

> *the coin can walk back down, but it never turns back over*

That is **hysteresis of habitus** - dispositions outlive the conditions that produced them. A
team that stops coordinating loses the Trust, but not the safety it learned to expect.

## Psychological safety, named properly
"Psychological safety" (Amy Edmondson) isn't one of this project's three named theorists,
but it's the real-world outcome the game is chasing: a team where people will challenge an
idea, admit a mistake, or ask a "stupid" question without fear. In this game, **Trust stands
in for psychological safety** - and it only moves through **Coordinate**, because safety
isn't declared, it's built through repeated, low-stakes engagement. That's exactly how
Wenger describes a community of practice forming.

Its clearest mechanical expression is **Trust ≤ 1 → Learn does nothing**: without safety,
knowledge will not move, no matter how many person-days you spend pushing it.

## One borrowed law, and where it stands
The **feature absorption cap** (§10) used to be **Brooks's Law**: adding people to a late
project makes it later. The cap was removed in rulebook v0.19 - the team may now pile every
day into one feature if it wants to, with no rule stopping it. Brooks's Law went without a
dedicated mechanic for one version. **v0.20 gives it one back**, in a different shape: the
**MORE HANDS** Event card (which replaced NEW TEAM MEMBER, now retired - Onboarding triggers
automatically off §13's Replacement rule instead) halves the Work on whichever feature got
the most piled onto it that week. It's occasional pressure rather than a permanent cap, which
is arguably closer to Brooks's own point: it's not that concentrated effort never works, it's
that *late* reinforcement carries a coordination tax. See
[Designer's Notes](./04-designers-notes.md).

### Open question - the Item is under-theorised
Every other part of the habitus figure now does specific theoretical work. The **Item** is
supposed to be Vygotsky's mediating artifact - the tool through which the character acts and
thinks - but mechanically it only grants one extra person-day per game.

Mediation says the tool should change *how* you work, not just let you work more. A truer
version would be something like: *your Item determines which Activity you may take on a day
when the team is under pressure*, or *your Item lets you convert one Activity into another once
per week.* Both are speculative. **Flagged, not applied** - this is exactly the kind of thing to
decide after a playtest shows whether the Item is memorable or forgotten.

## Why this matters for design
Every time you add a new Activity, Event, or Habitus effect, ask: which of these mechanisms
is it actually using? If the answer is "none, it's just a flavour bonus," cut it or redesign
it. See the habitus design rule in [habitus-model.md](./habitus-model.md) (strength +
tendency, never a flat bonus) - the same discipline applies here.
