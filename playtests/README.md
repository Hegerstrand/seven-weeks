## Session 1 plan (first playtest) - play **Lite**, not v0.1
The [playability assessment](../docs/playability-assessment.md) found v0.1 unplayable as
written (no numbers on most Activities, ambiguous action currency, a work budget using under
20% of capacity, and a deadlocked Capital loop). Session 1 therefore runs
[../rules/rulebook-lite.md](../rules/rulebook-lite.md): 15 days, 5 Activities, 2 tracks, no
Capital, all-paper components, 45-60 minutes.

Full session instructions are in Lite §11. In short:

- **Players:** 4, mixed experience - at least one person outside the hobby. (Testing only
  with hobbyists hides onboarding problems.)
- **Who teaches:** a named rules-explainer actively coaches Week 1 (days 1-5), then
  deliberately steps back for days 6-15. Note the moment help stopped being needed.
- **Build the habitus columns before explaining anything** (Lite §3) - five paper part-cards
  per player. Nothing is 3D-printed; the build ritual itself is under test.
- **What to build first:** habitus part cards, 4 feature cards, the Trust/Capability tracks,
  the day track, and the 12 Event cards. Roughly 20 minutes of index-card
  work.
- **Length:** play all 15 days. Lite is sized to finish.

**Answer these three, explicitly, in the log:** Q1 is a day under 3 minutes? Q2 was Work ever
*not* the obvious action? Q3 did the habitus build change anyone's behaviour? If Q2 comes back
"Work was always right," stop and fix the action economy before tuning anything else.

## What to listen for (don't just record outcomes - record what people say)
These are the moments that matter more than whether players can name the theory behind them:
- *"We actually had enough time. We just spent it badly."*
- *"We should have taught [X] that in week two."*
- *"We became really efficient, but when the requirements changed we were in trouble."*
- *"Nobody challenged him because he had all the status."*
- *"We delivered everything, but that team is completely burned out."*

## Debrief questions
- **Fun:** Did we enjoy making decisions? Did we feel pressure? Any memorable moments?
- **Strategy:** Were there real trade-offs? Was Work always the obvious choice? Were the
  non-Work activities worth the time?
- **Team dynamics:** Did Trust actually matter? Did sharing knowledge matter? Did Habitus
  visibly change how people behaved?
- **Theory:** Did the game feel anything like Wenger's community of practice? Did social
  learning actually matter? Did Habitus create real behaviour patterns?
- **Balance:** Is the project too easy or impossible? Are Events too harsh? Is onboarding
  worth the time? Is rest worth the time?
